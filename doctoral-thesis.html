01 / Research question

Access depends on more than formal coverage.

The dissertation studies how infrastructure, financing and provider organization jointly determine whether healthcare can be used effectively.

How do changes in public healthcare provision and institutional design affect maternal and infant health in settings characterized by institutional fragmentation and fiscal constraints?
01Availability

Infrastructure, staff and clinical resources.

02Accessibility

Distance, travel time and service location.

03Acceptability

Perceived quality, fit and willingness to seek care.

04Contact

Actual use of the healthcare system.

05Effectiveness

Care that responds to the patient's need.

02 / Three essays

Three institutional margins reveal different parts of access.

The chapters use distinct sources of variation, but follow one analytical logic: policy changes supply, household options and the location of care.

01Capacity2008–2017 02Financial coverage2015–2023 03Provider organization2015–2023
01

Capacity

Staggered difference-in-differences · Callaway and Sant’Anna

Do public hospital openings improve health?

The chapter compares 61 municipalities receiving their first local community hospital with 302 municipalities that remained without second- or third-level facilities.

+0.222points in APGAR
+26.2 ppdelivery in municipality of residence
−2.4 pphome births

The strongest evidence concerns immediate health at birth: APGAR improved, while birth weight did not change detectably. Earlier prenatal care and more institutional deliveries are consistent with improved access, although not every mechanism satisfies the same pre-trend tests.

What this demonstratesQuasi-experimental design, treatment construction, spatial spillovers, robustness analysis and disciplined interpretation of mechanisms.
02

Financial coverage

Difference-in-differences with continuous treatment intensity

Can infant-health gains persist without public insurance?

Prior municipal reliance on Seguro Popular identifies differential exposure to its elimination and replacement by INSABI.

+0.66 ppmacrosomia under full exposure
−24.5%real patient-directed funding
0detectable broad deterioration

The results do not show generalized deterioration in prenatal care, prematurity, APGAR, birth weight or infant mortality. They identify a robust increase in macrosomia, concentrated in states that fully adopted INSABI.

What this demonstratesContinuous-exposure designs, high-dimensional fixed effects, pandemic sensitivity analysis, institutional research and careful separation of treatment from mechanism.
03

Provider organization

Patient-flow outcomes · Continuous-exposure design

Losing coverage, changing providers

The chapter asks where childbirth moved after the reform across public networks, private hospitals, levels of care and home births.

−7.8 ppcommunity and rural hospitals
+4.4 ppIMSS-Bienestar
Preservedinstitutional childbirth

In states adopting INSABI, births shifted from Ministry of Health community and rural hospitals toward IMSS-Bienestar. The public network absorbed part of the change without a systematic shift toward home births or first-level clinics.

What this demonstratesProvider-network analytics, patient-flow decomposition, outcome taxonomy and interpretation under fragmented eligibility rules.

06 / Conclusions

Aggregate stability can conceal changes in access and who bears the cost.

The three essays support a shared interpretation of healthcare reform in a fragmented system.

01

Capacity and coverage are complementary

Community hospitals improved APGAR and proximity to institutional care, but did not produce detectable changes in accumulated fetal health measured by birth weight.

02

Stable averages do not imply an inconsequential reform

The rollback did not generate generalized deterioration, but macrosomia increased among the most exposed municipalities and provider flows changed materially.

03

Fragmentation also acts as an adjustment network

IMSS-Bienestar absorbed part of the decline in community-hospital deliveries, preserving institutional childbirth while potentially shifting travel, quality and household costs.

Analytical boundary

The models identify institutional changes, not every mechanism inside them.

The reform estimates are reduced-form effects of changes that combined financing, administration, eligibility and service delivery. Adoption was not random, COVID coincided with the transition, and SINAC does not follow mothers longitudinally. These limits shape the conclusions rather than appearing as footnotes after them.
