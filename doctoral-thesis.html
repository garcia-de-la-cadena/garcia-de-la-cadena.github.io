How coverage, capacity and provider organization shape maternal and infant health in Mexico.

01 / Research question

Formal coverage is only one part of effective access.

The dissertation separates three margins that determine whether healthcare is available, reachable and clinically useful.

How do changes in public healthcare provision and institutional design affect maternal and infant health in settings characterized by institutional fragmentation and fiscal constraints?
01

Availability

Facilities, staff and clinical resources.

02

Accessibility

Distance, travel time and service location.

03

Acceptability

Perceived quality and willingness to seek care.

04

Contact

Actual use of the healthcare system.

05

Effectiveness

Care that responds to the patient’s need.

02 / Three essays

One thesis, three institutional margins.

Each essay links a policy change to an explicit comparison group, a defined outcome family and a bounded claim.

01

Capacity

Do public hospital openings improve maternal and infant health?

The analysis compares 61 municipalities receiving their first community hospital with 302 municipalities that remained without second- or third-level facilities.

DesignStaggered difference-in-differences using Callaway-Sant’Anna ATT(g,t), municipality panels and extensive robustness checks.
Selected birth outcomes

Finding. The opening improved immediate neonatal condition measured by APGAR and shifted care toward institutional delivery, but it did not change accumulated fetal health measured by birth weight.

02

Financial coverage

Did infant-health gains persist after Seguro Popular was eliminated?

Pre-reform municipal reliance on Seguro Popular identifies differential exposure to the transition toward INSABI.

DesignContinuous-exposure difference-in-differences with municipality effects, state-by-quarter effects and pandemic sensitivity analyses.

03 / Methods

A complete chain from institutional change to defensible inference.

The value lies not only in estimation, but in linking administrative systems, defining exposure and matching each claim to its diagnostics.

01

Institutional research

Translate reforms, eligibility and provider governance into empirical margins.

02

Data engineering

Connect SINAC, CLUES, mortality, INEGI and CONAPO data across facilities, municipalities and quarters.

03

Identification

Construct staggered treatments or continuous exposure with explicit comparison groups.

04

Validation

Test pre-trends, alternative samples, spillovers, composition, migration and pandemic confounding.

05

Communication

Separate supported conclusions, plausible mechanisms and unresolved questions.

Analytical boundary

The reform estimates identify bundled institutional changes, not every mechanism inside them.

Financing, administration, eligibility and provision changed together. Adoption was not random, COVID overlapped with the transition, and SINAC does not track mothers longitudinally. These limits determine the wording of the conclusions.

05 / Conclusions

More infrastructure and institutional reform changed care, but effects differed by margin.

The three essays point toward a shared interpretation of access in a fragmented health system.

01

Capacity and coverage are complementary.

A nearby hospital can improve access and immediate neonatal condition, but infrastructure alone does not guarantee broader health gains.

02

Stable averages can conceal specific vulnerabilities.

The rollback did not produce generalized deterioration, yet macrosomia increased in the most exposed adopting settings.

03

Provider networks absorb institutional shocks.

IMSS-Bienestar partly replaced community-hospital deliveries, preserving institutional childbirth while potentially shifting other costs to households.
