01 / Decision question

Which hospitals spend differently after accounting for what they do and whom they treat?

Public budgets reveal total exercised expenditure. Discharge records reveal activity and case mix. The MVP connects both sources to estimate comparable—not accounting—resource intensity.

The objective is not to invent a price for every code. It is to explain expected expenditure and identify clinically interpretable deviations for further review.

01

What is observed

Exercised operating expenditure, patient episodes, diagnoses, procedures, hospital activity and capacity.

02

What is estimated

Base therapeutic-area intensity, incremental treatment-route signals and expected hospital expenditure.

03

What is not claimed

Exact patient-level costs, causal procedure effects, inefficiency or overpricing.

Positioning

A screening and benchmarking tool: it identifies where a hospital merits closer operational or accounting review.

02 / Data architecture

Start with episodes; aggregate only after the clinical structure is preserved.

The episode-level table constructs diagnosis–procedure relationships. The hospital-year table connects those relationships with expenditure.

Episode level

One row per inpatient episode

Hospital-year level

One row per institution and year

  • Discharges and treatment-route counts
  • Beds, staff, surgeries, visits and emergencies

Denominator rule

Use observed treated episodes—not population incidence.

Incidence measures population need. This expenditure model requires the patients actually treated by each hospital. Mixing both would confound need, access, referrals and utilization.

03 / Route discovery

Use Market Basket to discover patterns—then convert them into usable treatment routes.

Apriori or FP-Growth is applied separately within each therapeutic area. Association rules reveal co-occurrence; they do not by themselves define sequence, cost or mutually exclusive pathways.

Why the primary benchmark omits hospital fixed effects

Hospital indicators would absorb the differences the benchmark is intended to reveal. Repeated observations are handled through hospital-grouped validation and clustered uncertainty; a hierarchical random intercept can be reported as a sensitivity analysis.

Identification rule

Every therapeutic area needs an omitted reference route.

If total area cases equal the sum of every route count, including all routes creates perfect collinearity. The base coefficient represents the reference pathway; penalized route coefficients represent deviations from it.

05 / Validation

Selection is only useful if it survives outside the hospital that generated it.

The workflow emphasizes transportability and stability rather than a high in-sample fit.

01

Preprocess

Harmonize and standardize

Align expenditure definitions, deflate monetary values and standardize only penalized predictors.

02

Cross-validation

Leave complete hospitals out

Tune the penalty on institutions absent from training, avoiding leakage across years of the same hospital.

03

Stability

Bootstrap selected routes

Retain interactions that recur across resamples instead of relying on one arbitrary selection.

04

Interpretation

Refit after selection

Estimate a transparent post-LASSO or hierarchical model using the stable route set and report uncertainty.

06 / Decision outputs

A benchmark that points to questions—not verdicts.

Each output is designed for screening, comparison and budget dialogue.

01

Relative resource intensity

Normalize stable route coefficients against a clinically meaningful reference pathway.

02

Expected expenditure

Estimate expenditure conditional on scale, capacity, therapeutic mix and treatment routes.

03

Observed / expected ratio

Flag institutions above or below their comparable benchmark, with uncertainty.

04

Route stability atlas

Separate stable, emerging, disappearing and potentially recoded treatment patterns.

05

Budget expenditure per discharge

Report operating expenditure divided by discharges as a descriptive indicator—not an audited patient cost.

06

Geographic view with guardrails

Map hospital indicators without calling them territorial per-capita spending unless patient origin defines the catchment.

07 / Guardrails

What must remain visible when the benchmark is used.

Third-level budgets cover more than inpatient episodes, and referral hospitals do not serve only the population around their address.

Budget scope

Separate operating expenditure from investment and document payroll, teaching and research components where possible.

Aggregate identification

Coefficients are hospital-level budget associations, not audited patient-level costs or causal treatment effects.

Coding quality

Changes in classification, completeness or billing practice can resemble a clinical shift.

Performance language

A high observed-to-expected ratio prompts review; it does not by itself establish waste, inefficiency or overpricing.
