01 / Strategic frame

The market is organized around decisions—not isolated molecules.

A therapy becomes relevant only after subtype, stage, biomarker, treatment history and access conditions define a clinically coherent population.

The framework converts a complex treatment landscape into traceable decision rules that can support portfolio strategy, access research and competitive monitoring.

01

HR+/HER2−

Endocrine backbone, CDK4/6 inhibition and biomarker-directed sequencing across ESR1, PI3K/AKT, BRCA and HER2-low.

02

HER2+

Accumulated exposure to anti-HER2 mechanisms, with HR status, residual disease and CNS involvement changing the sequence.

03

TNBC

Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, PARP and ADC pathways shaped by PD-L1, germline BRCA and HER2-low status.

Taxonomy rule

HER2-low and HER2-ultralow are actionable labels—not a fourth primary subtype.

They remain nested within HR+/HER2− or TNBC and open eligibility for selected ADC strategies in defined disease settings.

03 / Treatment pathways

Three therapeutic clusters; six distinct early and advanced journeys.

Select a cluster to see the decisions that organize treatment. These are structured portfolio rules, not recommendations for an individual patient.

Early / resectable

Risk determines surgery-first, chemotherapy and adjuvant intensification.

  1. Surgery ± radiotherapy for resectable lower-risk disease.
  2. Endocrine therapy remains the adjuvant backbone.
  3. CDK4/6 or olaparib can intensify selected high-risk disease.
  4. Menopausal status, nodal burden, genomic risk and gBRCA change eligibility.

Advanced / metastatic

Endocrine sensitivity and acquired biomarkers organize sequencing.

  1. Endocrine therapy + CDK4/6 is the central first-line route outside visceral crisis.
  2. Progression triggers reassessment of ESR1, PI3K/AKT/PTEN, BRCA and HER2-low.
  3. SERD, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, PARP and ADC choices depend on prior exposure.
  4. Rapid progression or endocrine exhaustion opens chemotherapy and ADC routes.

Early / resectable

Neoadjuvant response creates the decisive postoperative branch.

  1. Small lower-risk tumors may proceed to surgery and taxane + trastuzumab.
  2. Stage II–III disease generally opens chemotherapy + dual HER2 blockade.
  3. pCR supports continuation; residual invasive disease opens T-DM1.
  4. HR+ disease adds endocrine therapy without replacing HER2 blockade.

Advanced / metastatic

Prior anti-HER2 exposure and CNS disease determine the next mechanism.

  1. First-line standards vary by geography and current label.
  2. T-DXd, pertuzumab and T-DM1 history must be explicit.
  3. Tucatinib + trastuzumab + capecitabine anchors an important CNS route.
  4. HR+ maintenance may combine anti-HER2 and endocrine treatment.

Early / resectable

Perioperative strategy is shaped by risk and pathologic response.

  1. Selected small tumors may begin with surgery.
  2. High-risk stage II–III disease opens platinum/taxane-based neoadjuvance with pembrolizumab where indicated.
  3. pCR and residual disease create different postoperative paths.
  4. gBRCA and prior ICI exposure condition olaparib or capecitabine decisions.

Advanced / metastatic

TNBC is biomarker-rich at the point of treatment selection.

  1. PD-L1 CPS, gBRCA and HER2-low should be visible before first-line selection.
  2. PD-L1 positivity can open pembrolizumab-based combinations.
  3. PARP, ADC and chemotherapy paths depend on biomarker and exposure.
  4. CNS disease and access to infusion create additional branches.

04 / Mexico access

Clinical eligibility and effective access are different outcomes.

The framework assigns a separate evidence state to registration, clinical recommendation, institutional inclusion, procurement and documented administration.

BClinical standardGuideline or PRONAM
CInstitutional inclusionFormulary or authorization path

Diagnosis and pathology

Time to imaging, biopsy, ER/PR/HER2 and repeat testing.

Precision biomarkers

Sample quality, test availability, payer and turnaround time.

Authorization and procurement

Institutional rules, budget, contracting, substitutions and partial delivery.

Administration and continuity

Infusion capacity, monitoring, transportation, adherence and persistence.

Access principle

A national guideline, institutional code or purchase record cannot be presented as proof that all eligible patients received treatment.

Regulatory rule

“Globally approved” is not a usable status.

Each event must retain the jurisdiction, date, indication, line and source. FDA approval is a global competitive signal, not proof of COFEPRIS registration or Mexican access.
