01 / The brief

Prevalence is not the treated market.

The commercial question was not simply how many people live with HS. It was how many could plausibly reach and persist on a biologic in Mexico’s public sector—and how the opportunity could be distributed across molecules with overlapping indications.

01

How many people live with HS?

Translate heterogeneous global evidence to Mexico’s age-sex structure.

02

How many are clinically visible?

Model onset, disease duration, diagnostic delay and severity.

03

How many reach treatment?

Make every public-access transition explicit and uncertain.

04

Which molecule could serve them?

Separate HS demand from competing indications and launch scenarios.

Decision use

The output is a planning range for opportunity assessment, patient-finding priorities and evidence-generation—not a claim of observed demand, sales or market share.

02 / Methodology

One connected model, five explicit modules.

Each module answers a different question and passes full uncertainty—not only a point estimate—to the next stage.

Observed

Direct study counts or population data.

Transported

Clinical parameters adapted from external populations.

Scenario assumption

Editable access priors where Mexican data are unobservable.

01

Evidence architecture

Keep unlike evidence separate

The primary likelihood uses 247 clinically confirmed cases among 22,743 participants across 23 GHiSA countries under one harmonized protocol. Telephone, claims and hospital studies from Latin America remain benchmarks because they measure different constructs.

23countries 22,743participants 247confirmed cases
02

Bayesian prevalence

Estimate risk by age and sex, then poststratify to Mexico

A binomial-logit hierarchical model combines country effects with regularized age and sex patterns. Each posterior draw is reweighted to Mexico’s 2024 population pyramid, then receives an explicit transportability effect for a country not directly observed.

p(MX) = Σ age,sex weight(MX) × risk(age,sex,country)
Observed Transported
03

Clinical-demographic journey

Link diagnosis to disease duration—not age alone

Synthetic profiles combine bimodal age of onset, current age and a log-normal time to diagnosis. Severity uses a discounted Dirichlet prior from community evidence. The model does not assume that every older patient is automatically severe.

Sensitivity tests diagnostic-delay means of 7, 10 and 14 years and alternative severity mixes.

Transported
04

Public access

Model the bottlenecks instead of inventing demand

Because no national series of negotiated demand, delivery, dispensing or private prescriptions by indication was observable, the model uses editable Beta-PERT priors for active disease, conventional failure, clinical compatibility, public coverage, specialist evaluation, initiation and persistence.

Scenario assumption
05

Molecule and indication

Allocate treated patients without dividing sales by indications

A Dirichlet scenario allocates persistent patient-years across adalimumab, secukinumab and bimekizumab. Separate indication pools estimate the potential HS share of units for multi-indication molecules. Current-evidence and hypothetical-launch scenarios remain distinct.

03 / Why this approach

The model is useful because its uncertainty is structured.

The objective is not complexity for its own sake. It is to prevent false precision while keeping the estimate actionable and easy to update.

01

Comparable evidence only

Avoids a naïve average of surveys, claims and hospital records that estimate different populations.

02

Mexico-specific structure

Uses Mexico’s age-sex pyramid instead of assigning disease randomly to a national total.

03

End-to-end uncertainty

Propagates 50,000 draws through prevalence, diagnosis, severity, access and molecule allocation.

04

Transparent assumptions

Labels what is observed, transported or assumed, so reviewers can challenge the right inputs.

05

Decision-ready outputs

Shows where patients are lost and which parameter would most improve the commercial estimate.

06

Designed to update

Demand, dispensing, prescriptions or expert validation can replace priors without rebuilding the model.

05 / Interpretation

What the result can—and cannot—support.

No national Mexican prevalence study

GHiSA evidence is transported through demographic poststratification and an explicit country uncertainty term.

Diagnosis and severity are proxies

Both are informed by non-Mexican cohorts, discounted for indirectness and tested in sensitivity analyses.

Access is prior-predictive

There is no verified national demand, delivery, dispensing or prescription series conditioning the public funnel.

Molecule mix is not market share

It is a scenario allocation of potential patient-years, not observed sales, procurement or treated patients.

Highest-value next evidence

Calibrate with institution-level demand, award and delivery by presentation.

The model is intentionally built so these observations can update the public-access priors. Prescription or patient counts by indication would be the second most valuable addition.
