Violence, Formal Employment and Local Capacity

Synthetic-control evidence on how localized homicide surges changed formal employment trajectories in three Mexican municipalities.

01 / Research question

How much formal employment is lost when violence suddenly escalates, and how long does the gap persist?

The paper separates the initial resistance of local labor markets from their subsequent recovery and interprets heterogeneity through the institutional context of each episode.

A homicide wave can interrupt mobility, business operations, hiring and investment. The employment effect may persist after lethal violence declines.

01

Directly estimated outcome

The monthly trajectory of formal jobs registered with the IMSS.

02

Contextual dimension

Municipal police capabilities and intergovernmental coordination before and during each crisis.

03

Identification boundary

Institutional capacity helps interpret heterogeneity, but the design does not identify its causal contribution.

02 / Three episodes

Similar labor-market shocks emerged from different violence dynamics.

The cases vary in intensity, duration, criminal origin and the public capabilities available to respond.

SEP 2017

La Paz

A territorial dispute met a local security structure with limited prior professionalization. Federal support became necessary during the escalation.

JUL 2017

Tepic

The episode followed the dismantling of a criminal-political network and changes in police coordination. The institutional setting cannot be reduced to a simple high- or low-capacity label.

SEP 2024

Culiacán

An internal cartel fracture overwhelmed a municipality that had expanded recruitment and police evaluations. The post-event horizon captures contraction, not recovery.

03 / Data and method

Each observed trajectory is compared with a weighted counterfactual.

The design searches for municipalities that reproduce the treated municipality before violence escalates, then measures the post-event gap.

01

Data

Monthly IMSS employment and homicide mortality

IMSS insured-worker records span January 2015 to July 2025. INEGI vital statistics and CONAPO population projections produce monthly homicide rates.

IMSSINEGICONAPO
02

Counterfactual

A donor pool with low and stable homicide rates

The donor pool contains 120 municipalities in 20 metropolitan areas. Treated states and municipalities with fewer than 1,000 formal workers are excluded.

12020 metros
03

Model selection

500 pre-event specifications per case

Predictor windows vary from one to twelve months. The preferred model retains structural information and minimizes pre-treatment RMSE.

RMSEPlacebos
04

Inference

Effects are judged relative to placebo trajectories

One-sided placebo p-values compare each treated gap with donor municipalities whose pre-treatment RMSE remains within the specified tolerance.

04 / Results

Violence opened persistent employment gaps in La Paz and Culiacán; Tepic remains inconclusive.

The magnitude and inferential strength differ across cases and horizons. Culiacán’s shorter follow-up does not permit an assessment of recovery.

05 / Healthcare connection

Formal-job loss can become a social-protection and healthcare-access shock.

Because the outcome counts workers insured through the IMSS, employment contraction may affect more than income. It can weaken an employment-linked route to healthcare and care benefits.

01Violence surgeObserved
02Operational and mobility disruptionPlausible mechanism
03Formal-employment contractionEstimated
04Employment-linked protection at riskInstitutional implication
05Potential healthcare-access barriersNot directly measured

The outcome already represents insured employment.

The study does not measure all municipal work. It follows permanent urban and rural workers plus temporary urban workers registered with IMSS.

Employment shocks can extend to household protection.

A formal job bundles earnings with access to employment-linked medical services and care benefits. Losing that job can therefore increase household vulnerability.

Economic recovery and violence control follow different clocks.

La Paz shows that homicide rates can decline while the formal-employment gap continues. Restoring security does not automatically restore employment-linked protection.

The healthcare channel remains an evidence gap.

The design does not observe loss of affiliation, service use, medical spending or health outcomes. Those variables are a clear next step, not a result of this paper.

Evidence boundary

The paper identifies a formal-employment effect, not a healthcare effect.

The healthcare relevance follows from Mexico’s employment-linked social-protection architecture and the definition of the IMSS outcome. It should be presented as a consequential access pathway that requires direct empirical testing.

06 / Demonstrated skills

The project connects causal evidence, institutional context and social protection.

It combines administrative labor data, mortality records, counterfactual construction and disciplined interpretation across three local crises.

01

Synthetic control

Counterfactual construction, donor selection, fit diagnostics and treatment-gap estimation.

02

Administrative data

Monthly IMSS employment linked with homicide mortality and metropolitan definitions.

03

Robustness

Placebos, alternative specifications, leave-one-out tests, donor restrictions and shifted dates.

04

Institutional research

Municipal police capacity and intergovernmental coordination used as context, not as an unearned causal claim.

05

Healthcare framing

Formal employment interpreted within an employment-linked healthcare and social-protection system.

06

Policy communication

Clear separation of direct findings, plausible mechanisms, limitations and actionable implications.
